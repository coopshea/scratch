There is a kind of cloud you have seen a thousand times and never thought about. Behind some jets the white line fades in a minute. Behind others it spreads into a sheet that hangs for hours. Those persistent ones are the problem, and [[contrails|they warm the planet]], by a share that is small in the global ledger and large for something nobody is trying to fix.

The mechanism is a blanket and a parasol. By day the sheet reflects a little sunlight. All day and all night it absorbs the infrared the Earth is trying to shed and sends half of it back down. The blanket is on twenty-four hours; the parasol only when the sun is up. So [[radiative-balance|the net is warming]], and for the flights that happen after dark there is [[night-vs-day|no argument at all]].

Where the sheets form is the interesting part. The air at cruise altitude is sometimes loaded with more water than it can hold, in thin pancakes a few hundred meters tall that can sit there for days. A jet flying through one is the grain of sugar dropped into rock candy syrup. Find [[issr|those layers]] and you have found the problem.

The fix is almost embarrassing. [[avoidance|Drop two flight levels]]. The layers are thin; go under them. It [[avoidance-cost|costs some fuel]], and that is where the story usually dies, because the airline pays the fuel and the world gets the benefit.

Except the benefit is not global. Unlike carbon dioxide, a contrail warms the region under it. [[incentives|The country that reroutes its own flights improves its own climate.]] That is the lever. It is the rare climate intervention where the actor and the beneficiary are the same government, and it can be [[make-the-case|verified from a weather satellite]]. Start with the night flights. Nobody argues about those.
